# Art direction

> Fictional brand made for the Specs demo.

## The camera

Shoot at the height and distance you'd naturally hold your phone to text someone a plant update. That's the reason the photos feel like a member's, not a studio's: the reader should think "I could take that."

- **Light:** window light or whatever the kitchen has. A yellow overhead bulb is fine.
- **Keep the mess that happened:** a leaning pot, leaning stems, dirt on the counter, the patch where nothing came up, a thumb holding a seed packet flat.
- **Soft is fine if it reads:** a soft frame works as long as you can still tell what is growing.
- **Don't add mess that didn't:** no grain filter, no fake film border, no vignette. Mess should come from what's in the frame, not an effect on top.
- **Vary the set:** in any group of three, aim for one soft shot, one crooked crop, and one fairly sharp one. That reads as one camera roll; the same effect on every image reads as a preset.
- **Leave packaging color alone.** Kraft cardboard and the slightly darker stamp on the box stay as shot. The palette is the intended ink, not a grade to apply.

## Photos and type together

The photo can be rough; the instructions can't. Put copy on cream, beside or below the photo, not over a busy part of the image. If text must sit on a photo, use soil-brown text on a cream label, like masking tape on a tub.

Any handwriting (a date, a circled step) is the post's one red element. See `visual-system`.

## The starting shot list

| # | Shot | What has to be in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The box just opened** | From above at a slight angle. The flap cuts into the frame; the instruction card is half tucked under a soil puck. |
| 2 | **Day six on the sill** | Pea shoots in a reused takeout tub, leaning toward the glass. A handwritten date on masking tape is visible. |
| 3 | **The first cut** | One hand with kitchen scissors, a small handful of shoots on a plate, the overhead light turning the counter a little yellow. |

Reference photos for all three are in `uploads/` (`photo-01-box-opened.jpg`, `photo-02-day-six-sill.jpg`, `photo-03-first-cut.jpg`).

**Shot 2 is the test of the direction.** You should be able to tell what's growing, how little room it takes, and what it looks like before there's anything worth putting on a plate. If a new photo can't pass that kind of read for its own moment, it's the wrong photo.

## Never show

- A yard, a raised bed, or a greenhouse. The members don't have one, and showing one breaks the promise.
- Perfect, styled harvests on marble. It sets a bar the first box can't meet.
- Stock-photo hands in gardening gloves with a trowel. Nobody needs a trowel for pea shoots.
