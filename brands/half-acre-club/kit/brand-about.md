# About Half Acre Club

> Fictional brand made for the Specs demo.

Half Acre Club sends one small growing project a month to people who don't have a garden. Most members rent. Most have a windowsill and not much else. Many have killed a plant before and quietly decided they're "not a plant person."

Each box is a single project, small enough to finish: the seeds, the growing mix if the project needs it, and a printed card to keep next to the tub. The card says how much room to clear, what light to look for, and what to expect on the days in between. Containers come from home; if a clean takeout tub will do, the card says what size to save.

The first box is pea shoots. Water until the soil feels like a wrung-out sponge, give it your brightest window, and turn the tub when the shoots start leaning. Around day ten, cut a handful with kitchen scissors. They taste a little like the inside of a fresh pea pod. Put them on eggs, in noodles, or eat one over the sink.

When a project needs more time or a brighter spot, the club says so before you order. When a batch stalls, it gives you one thing to check first. If you start over, you write a new date on the tape.

The box ships the first week of each month to the contiguous US, costs $24 a month, and can be cancelled anytime. Dana Reyes started it in Philadelphia, after filling her own windowsill past the point of reason.

What the club is really selling is the first win: the moment someone who thought they couldn't grow anything is standing at the counter with scissors and a plate.
