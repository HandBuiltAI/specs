# Voice core

> Fictional brand made for the Specs demo.

**The reference line:** "Pea shoots. Ten days. One windowsill. You've got this."

Every rule below explains why that line works.

## Rules

1. **Short sentences. Fragments are fine.** "Ten days." is a full thought. The reason: the reader is a beginner who's nervous about getting it wrong, and short lines feel doable.
2. **Lead with the concrete thing.** Name the plant, the number of days, the size of the space. "Pea shoots. Ten days." beats "Something fresh is coming this month." Specifics are what make a small project feel real.
3. **Encourage once, quietly, at the end.** "You've got this." closes; it doesn't open. One line of reassurance is warmth. Three is pressure.
4. **Talk to one person.** "You," not "you all" or "our community." Use "we" only for what the club does ("we ship the first week of the month").
5. **Plain words.** Say "sprout," not "germinate." Say "sunny window," not "south-facing exposure" unless the card explains it in the same sentence. The reason: jargon is the thing that made them feel like gardening wasn't for them.
6. **Small space is normal.** Never apologize for it ("even if you only have…"). State it as the setup: "One windowsill."

## Calibration

| Too far one way | On voice | Too far the other way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "This month we're growing pea shoots together! Grab a jar and let's get our hands dirty!!" | "Pea shoots. Ten days. One windowsill. You've got this." | "Pisum sativum microgreens. Harvest at 10 days." |
| Gushing, exclamation-heavy, "together" and "journey" | Calm, specific, one warm line | Clinical, catalog, no person in it |

## Never

- More than one exclamation mark in a post, and usually none.
- "Journey," "plant parent," "green thumb," or "hack." They are the clichés of the category.
- Guilt about dead plants. A dead plant is a normal first try, so mention it lightly, never as a failure.
